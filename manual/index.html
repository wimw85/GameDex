About Blyx Download

Blyx manual

Everything the app can do, with screenshots of the app itself. It runs on a Steam Deck, on a desktop and on an Android phone, and it reads Steam, PlayStation, or both at once. The screenshots are from the Deck; Phone and desktop lists what each one can and cannot do.

What Blyx is

Blyx is a second look at the games you already own. Steam and PlayStation show what you bought; Blyx shows what you can do with it: how many hours your backlog holds, what is nearly finished, what has not been touched since you bought it, and what on your wishlist is on sale right now. Both services land in one library, with a small mark on each card saying where a game came from.

Everything stays local

Your library lives in a database on the device itself. There is no server.

Works offline

Without a connection everything stays visible. Only updating has to wait.

Changes nothing in Steam

Blyx reads. All it asks Steam is: start this game, or install it.

Installing on a Steam Deck

  1. Switch to desktop mode.
  2. Download the latest Blyx.AppImage from the releases page and put it in ~/Applications.
  3. Make it executable: right click, Properties, Executable.
  4. Add it to Steam as a non-Steam game so you can start it in Game Mode as well.

Two settings on the shortcut

Compatibility off. Right click Blyx, Properties, Compatibility, and leave the box unticked. Proton runs Windows programs; this is a Linux one, and forcing a compatibility tool is the most common reason it refuses to start.

Launch options, if Game Mode stays black. Most installs need none, so try it without first. If Blyx opens on the desktop but shows nothing in Game Mode, put this in Properties, Shortcut, Launch options:

LD_PRELOAD= %command% --appimage-extract-and-run

Part What it does
LD_PRELOAD= Empties the variable Steam fills with its overlay library. That library is pushed into everything Steam starts and can stop a window from ever appearing under gamescope. You lose the Steam overlay inside Blyx, which has no use for it.
%command% Where Steam puts the program itself. Anything before it is environment, anything after it is an argument.
--appimage-extract-and-run Unpacks the AppImage to a temporary folder instead of mounting it through FUSE. Game Mode's session is a poor place to be mounting things.

You do not need --no-sandbox. Every build wraps its own executable so that flag is always passed. Adding it by hand changes nothing.

After that the app keeps itself current. When a new version exists it is fetched in the background and a small block appears in the rail with a button to restart into it. See Updates.

Connecting Steam

On first launch Blyx asks for your Steam account. On a Deck the app already knows who is signed in, so that is usually one confirmation. What it also needs is a Steam Web API key: you get one at steamcommunity.com/dev/apikey and paste it into the setup screen.

Mind your privacy setting. Steam only shares your library when Game details is set to Public. Set to private, an empty list comes back, and the app says so rather than pretending otherwise.

Then the first sync runs. It fetches your library, then achievements, completion times, prices and artwork. The first time takes a few minutes; after that it is keeping up rather than catching up.

Adding PlayStation

Settings has a tab for every account you have linked, and a + PlayStation beside them. Pressing it opens Sony's own sign-in page in a window of its own. Your password is typed to Sony; Blyx never sees it and keeps only the session Sony hands back afterwards.

After that, the first sync reads what you played and for how long, and your trophies per game. Sony hands over two lists that share no key, so Blyx asks a third question — which trophy set belongs to which game — rather than matching on titles, which used to miss games whose trophy set is filed under a different name.

What you get

Played games with hours, first and last played, trophies with rarity and unlock dates, and your trophy level with every platinum, gold, silver and bronze.

What is missing

No wishlist and no prices: Sony's mobile API refuses both, and no price service tracks consoles the way IsThereAnyDeal tracks PC shops.

Artwork

Sony ships one small icon per game, so covers, banners and logos come from SteamGridDB — and you can pick a different one yourself.

A game with no trophy progress at all has no trophy set on Sony's side, so Blyx shows none. That is Sony reporting on your account, not the game lacking trophies.

The rail

The rail is the app's steering wheel. Every entry is a question about your library with the answer beside it, so you can see what a filter would give you before you press it.

Entry What it shows
Backlog Everything unfinished and not hidden. The list you land on.
Perfect Games where you unlocked every achievement.
So Close Past 80 per cent but not complete. The list for an evening of finishing.
In progress Started, but not far along.
Finished What you marked as done yourself. Steam has no idea; you do.
Hidden What you put out of sight, plus whatever you hid on Steam itself.
Family Games somebody in your Steam family owns. Not yours, still playable.
Installed What is on this Deck right now.
Non-Steam What you added to Steam by hand: emulators, launchers, loose games.
Wishlist Your Steam wishlist, with prices. See Wishlist.

Exactly one entry is lit at any moment: the one you are looking at. At the foot of the rail sit Statistics, Settings and, in desktop mode, a button to close the app.

A card, read

Every game is one card, and every card says six things. They are always in the same place, so you can read them without reading.

1 2 3 4 5 6 7
  • 1Steam's own tall capsule. Where none exists you get a flat tile with a plus on it.
  • 2The name as Steam writes it.
  • 3How long the game runs according to HowLongToBeat. "No estimate" means no match was found, and you can make one by hand.
  • 4Time played on the left, when you last played on the right.
  • 5Achievements: the count and the percentage. "No achievements" means the game has none.
  • 6What it takes on disk, or would take if you installed it.
  • 7The button: Play when it is here, Install when it is not. While something is downloading this is the percentage instead.

A label can sit in the top right corner: Family for a game from your Steam family, Non-Steam for something you added yourself. A gold badge at the bottom left of the artwork means every achievement is unlocked.

Three densities

Three small buttons sit at the top right. They answer the same question three ways: how much of a game do you want to see at once. Your choice is remembered.

List

The densest of the three, for the thing a card is bad at: finding a title you already have in mind. The figures sit in fixed columns, so you can run your eye down one of them.

Cards

The default. Enough picture to recognise a game, enough text to choose one.

Large cards

The same card with more room. For when you are browsing rather than searching.

Searching and sorting

Search sits behind the magnifying glass. On a Deck that is deliberately a button rather than a field left open: as long as a text field exists anywhere, Steam keeps offering its keyboard. The field only exists while you are actually searching.

Sorting sits in the menu beside it. What is on offer depends on the list:

Size or download

Sorting by Size or download covers the whole library rather than only what is installed: the download size comes out of Steam's own cache and is known for very nearly every game you own.

Collections

The collections you made in Steam itself are here too. You can pick one or several at once, and you can have the list grouped with a heading per collection.

Hiding and marking finished

Two things only you know: whether you are done with a game, and whether you never want to see it again. Both sit in the menu you open with Y on a card, or with a right click.

Hidden games drop out of every list and every count except Hidden. They are also left out of the HowLongToBeat lookups, so hiding keeps the app quicker and the traffic smaller.

A game's own page

Open a card and you get the whole game. The wide artwork with its logo at the top, and everything the app knows below it.

Three actions sit at the bottom:

  • Look up completion times again asks HowLongToBeat once more.
  • Pick the HowLongToBeat entry lets you choose the right one.
  • Fetch artwork and achievements again re-downloads both.

Achievements

The second tab on a game's page. Unlocked ones carry their date, the rest carry what percentage of all players has them.

Steam leaves hidden achievements blank. Blyx fills those descriptions in from what SteamHunters knows, so you are not looking at an empty line.

Playing

The button on the card starts the game through Steam. On a Deck that works in Game Mode as readily as on the desktop; Blyx asks Steam to start the game and everything after that happens as it always does.

When the game is not here the button says Install. While something is downloading the card counts the percentage and turns into Play the moment it is in.

Installing, and which drive

A Deck has an internal disk and usually an SD card. Which of the two a game lands on is a real decision, and you make it here before the download starts rather than in a window Steam throws up afterwards.

If a game will not fit comfortably that line turns amber with not enough room. Blyx allows two gigabytes of headroom: a drive filled to the last byte is a broken Deck.

No SD card, no question. With only one drive, or when Steam cannot be reached, the panel skips itself and Steam installs the way it always did. You notice nothing.

Wishlist

Your Steam wishlist is a page of its own, with the same cards but different figures: no play time and no achievements, since you do not have those yet, but prices.

The button here says Store. It opens the shop holding the offer, inside Steam itself, because in Game Mode there is no other browser.

Prices and record lows

Prices come from IsThereAnyDeal, which keeps a price history across some fifty shops. That history is the point: 75 per cent off means nothing if the game was half that last winter.

What it says What it means
€12.49 The least you can pay for it anywhere today.
€49.99 What that same shop normally asks.
-75% The difference between those two.
All-time low €8.43 The lowest ever recorded, anywhere.
Lowest it has ever been Today's price matches or beats that record.

Only offers with a Steam key. Shops selling their own copy, such as GOG, Epic, Ubisoft, Microsoft, EA and Blizzard, stay out of view. Their prices are real, but the game would land in a library other than the one this app is about.

The app also remembers the lowest price it has ever seen itself, and records when something falls below it. That is not the same as a discount badge: shops announce a discount at every sale, this only speaks up when it is genuinely cheaper than anything we saw before.

Statistics

At the foot of the rail, or from Settings. Everything, added up.

Below that come the charts: played per month, played per hour of the day, and the value of your library at today's shop prices. That last one is emphatically not what you paid; what you handed over at the time lives on your Steam account page and nowhere an app can reach.

Fixing completion times

Completion times come from HowLongToBeat, matched on title. Usually that works, but not always: emulator entries carry the console's name, Pokémon games are filed without the word "Version", and a sequel is sometimes listed under a name the first game does not share.

Pick an entry yourself and it is pinned: the weekly refresh leaves it alone. You have given the answer, after all.

Backup to Google Drive

Everything this app knows and Steam does not exists in one place: what you hid, what you finished, your play history, the matches you fixed by hand. A Deck that dies takes it with it. That is what this backup is for.

  1. Press Sign in with Google and sign in.
  2. The first backup goes up straight away.
  3. After that, every hour while the app is open. The last 24 are kept.

The files land in a Blyx Backups folder in your own Drive, so you can get at them. Blyx asks for the narrowest access Google offers: files the app creates, and nothing else in your Drive.

Storage and cache

Artwork and achievement icons are kept on the device, so the app opens quickly and keeps working offline. How much that may cost is up to you.

Updates

The app checks for a new version itself and fetches it in the background. Once it is in, a small block appears at the foot of the rail with a button to restart. Nothing appears while there is nothing to report.

By hand works too: Settings, then Check for updates. The version you are running is written there as well.

Controls

The app is built for a Deck in your hands. A bar along the bottom always says what the buttons do at that moment, and it only names buttons Steam actually passes through.

Button Does
Stick or D-pad Moves the selection to whatever lies that way.
A Chooses what is selected.
B Closes a panel, or goes back.
X The shortcut on a game: Play, Install or Store.
Y The menu with hiding and marking finished.
Menu Opens the sort menu.
Two squares Opens search.

A keyboard does the same: the arrows move, Enter chooses, Esc goes back, and X, Y, M and F do what the table says. The mouse keeps working and the two never fight.

Steam's keyboard. To type in Game Mode, open it with Steam + X. Blyx never asks for it, because a window that summons a keyboard uninvited keeps doing so.

Offline

Without a connection the whole app stays usable: your library, your figures, the artwork, the achievement lists and the sizes are all on the device. What cannot happen is updating, and the app says so rather than pretending.

Playing and installing keep working as long as Steam itself is running; those two go straight to Steam rather than through Blyx.

Where the data comes from

Almost nothing on these screens was collected here. Your library is Valve's, the completion times were submitted by players, the price history has been kept by others for years.

The full list is in the app under Settings, at Thanks & credits: who provides what, whose trademarks are whose, and which open-source packages this is built from.

When something goes wrong

The library is empty after connecting

Almost always the privacy setting: Game details has to be Public in your Steam profile. Change it and run the sync again.

A game has no artwork

Not every game has every size on Steam. Open the game and press Fetch artwork and achievements again. If it stays empty the image does not exist, and the app shows a flat tile rather than a broken picture.

The completion time is wrong

Matching happens on title. Pick the right entry through Fixing completion times; your choice is never overwritten afterwards.

The keyboard keeps coming back

Close it with B and leave the search field. If it still returns, something has changed in Steam itself; write down what you did, because that is exactly the kind of thing that got solved before by measuring it.

Something went wrong during a sync

Settings, then What went wrong. It lists what failed, with the time beside it.

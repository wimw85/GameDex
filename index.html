The problem

A library of 2,000 games is not a library. It is noise.

You scroll past hundreds of titles you picked up in a sale, nothing stands out, and you start the same game for the sixth time. Not because there is nothing better in there, but because you cannot see what is in there.

Blyx splits your library along the questions you actually ask. What have I not started? What is nearly done? What have I not touched in months? Every answer sits in the rail with its count beside it, before you press anything.

  • Backlog everything unfinished, and not hidden
  • So Close past 80 per cent: the list for an evening of finishing
  • Perfect every achievement unlocked, and worth being proud of
  • Family what you can play through your Steam family without owning it

Time, not titles

How long does this actually take?

Every card carries how long the game runs according to HowLongToBeat, submitted by players who kept track of their own playthrough. Add that up across your backlog and you have a figure worth something.

Two hours on a Tuesday evening? Sort by length and take something that fits. Holiday coming up? Go find the forty-hour game you have been circling for three years.

The drive

Internal or SD card, asked before it is too late

On a Deck this is a real decision: the internal disk is fast, the card is large. Steam asks afterwards in a window of its own, or does not ask at all. Blyx asks where the decision is made, with the free space on each drive beside it.

If a game will not fit comfortably, the app says so before you start rather than twenty minutes into the download.

Deck, desktop and phone

The same library, cut to fit the screen it is on

Questions

What people want to know

Does it cost anything?

No. No price, no subscription, no adverts, no paid extras. It is a personal project.

Do I need an account?

Not with us. You connect your own Steam account with a key you get from Steam itself. If you want a backup in Google Drive you sign in with Google, and that part is optional.

Where is my data?

On your device, in a database belonging to the app. There is no server of ours involved. The only place anything is sent is your own Drive, if you connect one.

Does it change anything in my Steam library?

No. Blyx reads what Steam publishes and what the Steam client stores on your device. All it ever asks Steam to do is start or install a game. Hiding and marking things finished happens inside Blyx only.

Does it work in the Deck's Game Mode?

Yes. Add it to Steam as a non-Steam game and it starts like anything else. The whole interface is built for the controller.

Where do the completion times come from?

From HowLongToBeat, submitted by players. If a match is wrong you pick the right entry yourself, and that choice is never overwritten afterwards.

And the prices?

From IsThereAnyDeal, which tracks prices across some fifty shops including the lowest a game has ever been. Only offers that hand over a Steam key are shown.

What happens without internet?

Everything keeps working except updating. Your library, your figures, the artwork and the achievement lists are all on the device.

Your backlog is not getting any shorter.
